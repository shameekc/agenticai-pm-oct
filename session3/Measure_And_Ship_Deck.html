§2 · Judging the judge

Who evaluates the evaluator?

1

Specific criteria

"Was it good?" isn't a criterion. "Classification is one of exactly six values" is.

2

A score and a reason

The score says pass or fail. The reason says what to fix. Without it, a fail is noise.

3

A different model

A judge from the same model shares the agent's blind spots, and tends to agree with it.

4

Test the judge

Run it on 20 cases you've labelled. Below 85% agreement with you, the judge is the problem.

Code checks first (class, length, schema). A judge only where code can't decide. Lab 8: watch agreement collapse with the Miscalibrated judge.

§2 · Where does 0.80 come from?

A threshold is a price, not a feeling

False positive

Escalate when it wasn't needed

A human handles it: ~₹85 and a slightly slower reply.

False negative

Miss the ₹45K laptop complaint

An automated reply to a customer about to go to the consumer commission. CSAT, churn, legal exposure.

FN costs 10–50× more than FP
→ ESCALATE recall ≥ 95%, precision ≥ 70%
Pick the threshold that minimises: FP cost × FP count + FN cost × FN count

Calibration: label 100+ cases → run at 0.60 / 0.70 / 0.80 / 0.90 → precision, recall, review rate at each → cheapest total wins. Lab 9.

§4 · Build 5

Four guardrails, four doors

Input validator

What comes in

Injection, spam, missing order ID or email, tickets over 5,000 characters.

Output filter

What goes out

No refund amount, date or replacement promise. No PII beyond the order number. ≤ 75 words.

Topic boundary

What it may discuss

No competitors, internal information or legal proceedings.

Confidence gate

When to hand over

Below 0.80 → a human. Claim over ₹10,000 or safety → a human, whatever the score.

Test in this order: Ticket D (naive injection) → TKT-016 (injection hidden in a product name) → TKT-012 (spam) → Ticket B (no order ID). Then try to sneak "₹18,500 by Friday" past the output filter.

§4 · Discuss

Guardrails fail in both directions

Ownership

PM or legal?

The PM drafts, legal reviews, the PM owns the final spec in the PRD. "Legal writes it" means it never ships.

False positive

It blocks a real customer

A ₹400 refund question; the filter blocks any reply with an amount. What does the customer see? What's the fallback?

Measure it

Too strict?

Run the golden set with guardrails on and off. The drop in pass rate is the guardrail's own false-positive rate.

§5 · Four levers

Know where the money goes first

Model per step

$1,400 → $1,200

Haiku for classify and route. Smaller than you'd think: the draft's output tokens are most of the bill.

Prompt caching

−$300 / month

The 800-token system prompt is identical on every ticket. Cached reads: $0.10/M instead of $2/M.

Prompt length

Paid on every ticket

Every word in the system prompt is billed 200,000 times a month. Count tokens in prompt reviews.

Output cap

$1,400 → $3,800

Drop "≤ 75 words" and the draft runs 4× longer. A bigger swing than any model choice.

Do now (5 min): a smaller Wren brand at 2,000 tickets/day. Tokens per ticket, model per step, caching on or off, monthly cost, × 3. Lab 14 to check it.

§6 · Human in the loop

Three ways to put a human in

Review before output

Human approves first

High-stakes escalations.

Spec: what the reviewer sees · decision SLA
Review after output

AI acts, humans audit a sample

Standard auto-resolutions.

Spec: sampling rate · audit log format
Review on exception

A rule pulls a human in

Confidence < 0.80, claim > ₹10,000, safety.

Spec: exception rules · alert path · queue

The bottleneck: 200,000 tickets a month × 15% needing review = 30,000 human reviews. The reviewer's screen is a product. Spec it.

§6 · The AI control plane

Engineering builds it. You set the numbers.

Monitoring

Accuracy, escalation rate, confidence mix, response time, CSAT

Alerting

Accuracy −5% in 24 h · escalations +30% · CSAT < 3.5

Rollback

Fall back to the human queue, tell customers the SLA, name who decides

Circuit breaker

Injection in > 5% of tickets in an hour → auto-disable, page the PM

The PM owns

Alert thresholds · review-queue SLAs · exception criteria · the rollback call · recovery criteria · customer comms during an outage

Engineering owns

Instrumentation · alert delivery · the circuit breaker · the rollback button · logs · dashboards

The failure mode: "engineering will pick the thresholds." They pick numbers that feel right, and nobody notices the bad agent for three days.
